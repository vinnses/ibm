## Statement preserved in substance

The stakeholder does not consider 2,700 hours a target that should be followed
faithfully if reaching it removes important disciplines. They expect that the
Biological Sciences Sector will not open new course-specific disciplines. They
consider dialogue with the Health Sciences Sector possible, but do not expect
it to accept workload reductions even though its components may become leading
candidates in a workload-reduction exercise.

Because the reorganization is explicitly oriented toward artificial
intelligence, the stakeholder proposes using the fourth edition of Stuart
Russell and Peter Norvig's *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach* as a
coherence reference. Their intended curricular reading uses six broad parts so
that AI education is not reduced to currently fashionable model families. They
suggest that an introductory AI component could combine the first two parts;
machine learning and deep learning could be separate components; and vision
and language could also be separate. Their central normative position is that
an AI-rich curriculum cannot abandon mathematical and computational
foundations needed to build sound models, optimize resources, and understand
system behavior.

The stakeholder explicitly asks that W035 not optimize or finalize the total
workload now, because the desired educational content cannot yet be reconciled
responsibly with the proposed reduction.

5. Can health application be integrated through projects, data, ethics, and clinical contexts without silently weakening domain competence?

7. What prerequisite graph is needed before machine learning, deep learning, language, vision, and resource-aware deployment?

## Analytical guardrail

The testimony may guide advocacy and scenario design but cannot be cited as an
institutional fact. A later argument may say “we adopt this as a negotiating
constraint” or “the stakeholder reports this expectation”; it may not say that
a sector formally refused or accepted a change without independent evidence.
