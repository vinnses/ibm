## Statement preserved in substance

The stakeholder's view is that departmental interest in funding and two additional faculty positions may be driving the 2026 proposal; that the department and faculty historically gave insufficient attention to the course; that the 2011 creation may itself have been linked to calls producing unilateral departmental gains; and that an organized student/alumni collective now intends to advocate for the course's best interests.

The stakeholder identifies themself as already graduated and wishing to contribute to the current student collective's effort. No personal identifier is stored here.

## Testable research questions

1. Which edital, program, budget line, or institutional incentive preceded creation of the course around 2011?
2. What resources, faculty positions, infrastructure, or budget were requested, promised, allocated, and actually used at creation?
3. Which bodies proposed and approved the original course, and what reasons appear in minutes, opinions, feasibility studies, and the original PPC?
4. How were responsibilities distributed among DInf, other departments, the course coordination, and sector governance over time?
5. What contemporaneous records exist for staffing, teaching allocation, vacancies, course governance, student support, retention actions, and curriculum maintenance?
6. For the 2026 proposal, are the two faculty positions a precondition, a requested benefit, a documented academic need, an authorized allocation, or only an expectation?
7. Do meeting records show alternative solutions considered for demand, dropout, completion, curriculum, staffing, or course identity?
8. What participation did students, alumni, NDE, Colegiado, departments, and sector councils have in the proposal and deliberations?
9. Which measurable benefits and burdens would accrue to students, the course, participating departments, and the institution under each documented option?

## Evidence needed

- original creation edital/call, proposal, feasibility study, PPC, minutes, opinions, authorization and recognition acts;
- historical and current budget/resource allocation records tied to the course;
- faculty-position requests, authorizations, appointment/provision records, and teaching-allocation data;

- student-representation records and consultation materials;
- matched administrative outcomes and documented intervention timelines;
- the complete 2026 process, matrix, transition plan, staffing/resource commitments, result, and later acts.

## Analytical guardrail

The hypothesis must not determine source selection or convert documentary absence into intent. Evidence supporting, weakening, or contradicting it must be preserved with equal care. Motive should be stated only at the evidentiary level the sources permit: documented rationale, inference, stakeholder interpretation, or unresolved hypothesis.
